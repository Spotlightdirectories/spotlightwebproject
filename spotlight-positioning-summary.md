# Spotlight Directories — Positioning Summary

*Prepared August 2026, based on everything currently built on the platform.*

## Where Spotlight Stands Against the Competition

Nigeria's business-discovery space is currently split between three kinds of players, and Spotlight is built to sit above all three. Jiji is a classifieds site that added business accounts on top — huge traffic, but the trust layer is thin: no identity checks, generic star ratings that are easy to game, and no safety mechanism for a customer inviting a vendor to their home or inviting themselves to a vendor's workshop. VConnect is closer in spirit to Spotlight, with reviews and a "Request-a-Quote" flow, but it stops at the review level; there is no verification tier, no document-backed badge system, and no tool that helps a listed vendor understand how they're actually ranking against competitors. Connect Nigeria's quote-request model is useful for comparing like-for-like jobs, but again, trust is self-reported and there is nothing resembling growth analytics for the vendor side. Beyond these three, most small businesses and artisans in Nigeria are still discovered through Google Business Profile listings or plain Instagram/Facebook pages, neither of which was designed for niche trade discovery, safety, or lead tracking at all.

Spotlight's edge is not any single feature, it's that the platform treats trust and safety as core infrastructure rather than an afterthought. Every vendor can carry a Gray or Blue verification badge, each backed by real document upload and admin review, not just a checkbox. On top of that sits a recommendation system modeled on how Upwork and Fiverr build trust globally, but adapted for Nigeria's informal and artisan trade: a recommendation is tied to a specific completed piece of work in the vendor's portfolio, not a floating star rating anyone can leave. And uniquely among Nigerian directories, Spotlight has a built-in safety flow for physical visits — when a customer wants a vendor to come to their location (or vice versa), the platform requires a verified name and phone number to be confirmed on-platform first, directly addressing the real fear both sides have about meeting a stranger from a listing. No competitor reviewed here has anything comparable.

The other structural advantage is depth of discovery. The taxonomy behind Spotlight's search runs to 178 categories and 2,318 subcategories, which means a customer looking for a specific kind of artisan or service finds a precise match instead of being dumped into a broad bucket like "Repairs" or "Fashion." Combined with a searchable, type-ahead filter experience sitewide, this is a materially finer-grained discovery experience than any of the platforms above offer.

Finally, Spotlight doesn't stop at listing. Vendors get Business Insights and Marketplace Ranking tools that show them exactly where they stand in their category and subcategory, a Growth Coach that recommends concrete next steps, and Lead Alerts that notify them the moment a customer clicks WhatsApp, calls, or views a listing. None of the competitors above give a vendor any visibility into their own performance — Spotlight turns the listing into an ongoing growth relationship, not a one-time directory entry.

## Full Offering — What Spotlight Does, A to Z

**For vendors.** A business can sign up free or on a paid plan (billed monthly or yearly through Paystack), and list either products, services, or both from the same account if the business is hybrid. Each business can run multiple branches under one profile, upload photo and video galleries, and get an AI-assisted first draft of their business description. Listings carry a Portfolio section where finished jobs can be logged and — this is the differentiator — customers can leave a verified recommendation tied to that specific piece of work. Vendors can apply for a Gray or Blue verification badge by submitting ID, CAC, utility bill, and other documents for admin review, and can pay to get sponsored placement in search and category results. Once live, a vendor gets a full dashboard: Business Insights showing traffic and engagement over time, a Marketplace Ranking screen showing exactly where they sit against others in their category and subcategory, a Growth Coach with tailored recommendations, Lead Alerts for every WhatsApp click, phone call, and profile view (delivered by email and in-app, plus a weekly performance report), and a Visit Requests inbox for handling customer visit requests safely.

**For customers.** Anyone can search and filter listings by category, subcategory, state, and local government area, using the same searchable, type-ahead filters throughout the site rather than long unsearchable dropdowns. Customer accounts let people save favourite vendors, leave reviews, track their own visit request history, and reach a vendor directly by WhatsApp or phone from the listing. Before any in-person visit — whether the vendor is coming to the customer or the customer is going to the vendor — the platform runs a safety flow that confirms both parties' identity and contact details on-record first. A dedicated Safety Center explains these protections and gives practical guidance for meeting vendors safely.

**For partners.** Spotlight also runs a Partner Program for people who want to refer businesses onto the platform for a commission. Partners get their own signup and login, a dashboard that tracks referred vendors and commissions owed, and a full terms/legal reference page covering how the program works.

**Behind the scenes.** An admin dashboard runs the whole operation: staff accounts with role-based permissions, a security/audit log of sensitive actions, and approval queues for payments, sponsorships, verifications, and partner applications, each with its own history view. The platform automatically screens new listings for restricted or prohibited items before they go live. Every vendor, product, and service page carries its own dynamic SEO title and description pulled from real listing data, rather than one generic tag repeated across thousands of pages. The whole site supports dark mode, and — because production and the new Next.js rebuild share one live Supabase backend — the migration to the new site is happening without disrupting anything vendors or customers are doing on the platform today.
